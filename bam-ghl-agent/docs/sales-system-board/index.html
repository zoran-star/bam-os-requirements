The ways out of a stage and where each one leads. Every hand-off between engines happens at an exit point - that's the only place it's defined.

ENGINE TYPE

Agents (AI)

An AI that reads the whole conversation and writes the next message itself, using the academy's real info. One voice and one craft for every academy - lessons taught anywhere sharpen everyone. It can move leads between stages and book trials. Nothing sends without approval.

ENGINE TYPE

Automations

Pre-written message sequences on a timer. We seed each academy's sequence tailored to them at onboarding - after that the copy, timing and steps are theirs to change. The moment a lead replies, the automation stops and an agent takes over.

Ghosted drip (day 1/2/3 texts) · Nurture drip (+1/+2/+3 week emails) · instant confirmations.

CONTENT SAFETY

Sync class

Every automated message carries a class saying whether its words may travel to another academy: shared, local or attributed. Strictest wins, and anything unrecognised fails to the strictest. This is the mechanism that stops academy #4 sending another academy's parent quotes under its own name.

A step carrying real parents' words is attributed and never copies. Full explanation on BUILDING THE NEXT ONE.

Attached to both presets in the registry today. A new sales system gets it by existing.

Everything this sales system needs to know

This is the answer to "what do we have to get from a new academy before we can turn this on". Every blank in every message, every page and every agent reply pulls from one of the rows below. Three columns: what it is, where it is used, where it is collected.
The rule · Zoran, 30 July 2026

All of this data is MANDATORY before the sales system can be turned on for an academy. Not "nice to have", not "fill it in later". Every row on this page, collected and checked, before the switch.

The reason is that the system degrades politely. A missing link removes its own line, a missing schedule sends nothing at all, a missing coach list makes the agent vague. Nothing errors, nothing alerts, and a shorter message reads exactly like a correct one. That is why an absent fact has to be caught by a checklist before launch and cannot be caught afterwards.

No fact, no output - and that is exactly why the checklist is mandatory

A missing bare link takes its whole line, plus any dangling lead-in above it ("here's the link:"). A missing link inside a sentence takes only that sentence, so the rest of the message survives. A missing schedule or venue takes its own heading with it. If the whole body resolves to nothing, the send is refused rather than an empty text going to a provider.

This is good engineering and a bad safety net. It means an academy launched with half this page blank will send messages that look completely normal and are quietly missing their booking link, their review ask and their reply-to address.

Onboarding a new academy: five skills, in order

A skill is a build recipe our team runs. Each one fires when the owner finishes the step that feeds it, and the academy's Slack channel gets the exact skill to run. Staff runs it and workshops the result locally; the owner never sees a work-in-progress. The whole target is about 30 minutes of the owner's time.
The owner approves twice, and only twice

Their brand board, and their sales messages. Everything else our team approves in the workshop session.

What they approve on the messages screen is provably what sends: the approval page, the staff review and the real send all run the same rendering code, with a committed test holding them together.

The order, and why it is this order

Each one names what it needs from the one before, so the sequence is an argument rather than a list.

Fires when the owner submits their brand brief

Reads their old website and whatever branding assets they entered, then takes a pass at their brand with a staff member: colors, fonts, logo treatment. The output is their theme plus a one-page brand board published to their staging site.

Why it is first: the owner approving that board is what releases everything below it. Every page, every email shell and every funnel wears this theme, so building any of them before the theme is settled means building them twice.

Contacts and texting travel together, and that is not a choice we get to make

Building the next sales system

Read this page before you touch anything. It is the difference between a second sales system and 47 academies whose pipelines slowly stop resembling each other.
The rule

The SKILL forks per sales system. The machinery underneath it NEVER does.

"One client needs X" has exactly two valid answers: everyone gets X, or X becomes a runtime fact read off that academy's own record. There is no third answer. A per-academy branch in shared code is how one academy's needs become every academy's bug, and it is not reversible - once two academies have forked structures, no master edit can reach both.

The two things that mean you have gone wrong

Copying the preset registry file. Writing a condition on a specific academy's id. Either one means stop and pick one of the two valid answers above.

Sync class: whether a message's words may travel

Every automated message step carries a class. Anyone authoring content for a new sales system has to understand this, because nothing else detects a wrong answer.

Least strict
shared

Belongs to nobody. Generic copy: a nudge to pick a time, a last-call email. Copies to any academy untouched, and nobody reviews it. 24 of the 34 live steps.

Stricter
local

Authored for one academy. It would render fine anywhere, but the words are that academy's own account of itself: its story, how it trains. Never copied. 7 of 34.

Strictest
attributed

Carries real people's words. Never travels, under any circumstances. 3 of 34.

How it resolves

attributed > local > shared · strictest wins · unknown fails closed

A step's real class is the strictest of two things: the class written on the step itself, and the class of the shared template its body points at. Because the strictest wins, a step can only ever make its content stricter. An academy, a bad seed, or a form that forgets to send the field cannot downgrade an attributed template by writing "shared" on the row.

Anything unrecognised - a typo, a template key nobody declared - resolves to attributed, the strictest. That direction is chosen on purpose: a wrong "shared" is the exact bug this exists to prevent, while a wrong "attributed" only blocks a copy that somebody can then unblock by declaring the key.

The real example: the testimonials email

Until 29 July that email had real parent quotes written into the template, attributed by a city variable. Whichever academy sent it claimed those parents as its own. It is now built from a token that pulls each academy's own approved quotes, so the template itself carries nobody's words and is technically safe to copy.

It is still marked attributed anyway, and that is the instructive part. The class does two jobs: it gates copying, and a drift checker watches every attributed step to catch an academy sending quotes it does not own. Relaxing the class would quietly switch that watchman off. So the class stays strict until the coupling is dealt with properly. Do not "correct" a class by reading the template's current text - the copy changes, the decision about who owns the content does not.

What that table proves

A second sales system costs one extra stage, one extra calendar and two new agent missions. Zero new machinery, zero migrations, zero per-academy code, zero database template rows. That is what "locked underneath" buys.

And one honest finding from measuring it: the discovery-call preset declares no qualifications, so anything reading its contents gets an empty list where free trial returns three. Whoever ships it has to add them. Found while checking this page, not previously written down.

Two real failures, both from this month

Constraints are easier to remember as consequences.

One academy's review link inside everybody's agent

What happened: the social-proof section of the shared agent structure contained a literal line, "Google Reviews:" followed by BAM GTA's link. It sat in the structure every academy's agent is built from. Nothing overrode it, because that section had no renderer and 0 of 47 academies had a stored override. San Jose, DETAIL Miami and Next Level all pointed parents at a Toronto academy's review page.

Why: a default that names one academy is a default every other academy serves.

What prevents it now: that section's shared body is empty and stays empty, and the content is rendered per academy from its own quotes and its own review link. An academy with no reviews now says nothing about reviews. Verified on 30 July: still 0 stored overrides, and only 1 of 47 academies has a review link on file, so the honest reading is that the leak is fixed and the data is missing.

DETAIL Miami's board went blind

What happened: on 8 July its pipeline was flipped to the portal after a clean reconcile of 49 open leads. GoHighLevel workflows still owned lead creation, so the portal never heard about a new website lead, and the portal write that moves a card silently does nothing when the card is not in the store. Every new website lead was invisible on the board. Rolled back to GoHighLevel the same hour.

Why: reconciling the leads that already exist says nothing about who creates the next one. It was a green check that could not fail.

What prevents it now: the flip refuses on five named conditions - dual-write was never on, the store is empty, the stage registry was never seeded, the registry is only partly seeded, or there is real drift - and it recomputes the reconcile itself rather than trusting a passed-in result. A rollback to GoHighLevel is always allowed and instant. The honest gap: none of those five checks asks who owns lead creation. That is still a human check before every flip, and it is the check that would have caught this one.
