The five build skills,
and why in this order.

Each skill fires when the owner finishes the step that feeds it, and the academy's Slack channel gets the exact skill to run. Staff runs it and workshops the result in localhost; the owner never sees localhost. The owner approves twice in the whole process, and only twice: their brand board, and their sales messages. Everything else our team approves in the workshop session. Every stage below says what it needs from the one before and what it hands over to the next, so the order is an argument rather than a list.

Your goal

A new academy fully onboarded in about 30 minutes of their time, with the skills doing the human-judgement work and staff approving it.

Top to bottom. Every trigger already fires in production today.

The switchover: seeding and flipping are two different moments

Stage 3 seeds. The flip happens later, once the sales system is approved and actually sending, so there is never a window where neither system is handling their leads.

ContactsGoHighLevelstays on GoHighLeveltexting needs it, see below
Contacts and texting travel together, and that is not a choice we get to make

GoHighLevel can only text a person it holds a contact record for, and their replies only come back to us through that same record. So the moment contacts move to the portal, texting stops working for every new lead. Since you want them keeping their number and their texting, contacts stay put too. They are still fully copied into the portal and visible there, just not in charge.

DETAIL Miami already runs exactly like this, so this is a proven shape rather than a theory. BAM GTA is the only academy fully off GHL, and that is because it moved its texting to its own number.

Their old leads do not get texted, ever, unless you say so

Seeding brings across every lead they already had. San Jose alone has more than 65. If the sales system switched on over the top of them, it would start messaging people who signed up months ago. Seeded leads are quarantined by default and releasing any of them is a deliberate act with a name on it.

So the build list is four things

Build A
The sales system skill

The gold steps in stage 4. The funnel-page half exists and folds in; the messages half is new and drives the machinery built and locked this month.

Build B
The owner approval step

One new step in their wizard. They see every message exactly as a parent gets it, and approving is what switches the system on. What they approve is provably what sends.

Build C
The migration skill

Stage 3. Mostly drives tools that already exist, plus one real repair: the safety check that is meant to confirm both systems agree has never actually worked, so today the only way to flip is to skip it.

Build D
A re-run guard

Rebuilding a site currently replaces its pages wholesale, which would quietly eat any client-requested fixes made since. It now stops and lists them instead.

What the owner approves is what sends

The approval page, the staff review and the real send all run the same code, with a committed test proving it. The mismatch found and fixed this month cannot quietly return.

A skill that fires early does nothing, loudly

Each one says what it does when the stage before it is missing or half-done: it declines and explains. It never throws, and never builds from another academy's data.

The flip refuses rather than guesses

An academy flipped once before while its old system still owned new leads, and every new lead went invisible. The flip now checks that first and backs out rather than proceeding.
